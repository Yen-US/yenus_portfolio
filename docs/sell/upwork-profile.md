## 1. The positioning decision

Use one focused profile:

> **A senior AI engineer who can architect and ship reliable LLM systems.**

Do not lead with "consultant," "technical strategy," or a long list of frameworks. Those work on an owned consulting site where a buyer has time to understand the practice. On Upwork, the title and first two lines need to match the concrete problem a client is already trying to hire for.

The profile should sell this combination:

1. **Hands-on delivery:** OpenAI integrations, RAG, agents, evals, guardrails, APIs, and Azure.
2. **Senior judgment:** architecture, production failure modes, latency, cost, privacy, and migration risk.
3. **Proof under constraints:** a full production migration in seven days with zero customer-visible downtime.

### Best-fit jobs

- Productionizing an existing LLM, RAG, or agent prototype
- Fixing unreliable retrieval, tool calls, structured outputs, or fallbacks
- Building an OpenAI-backed feature into a Next.js or Python product
- Adding evaluations, observability, guardrails, model routing, or cost controls
- Azure OpenAI, Azure AI Search, or broader Azure AI architecture
- Vercel/GCP-to-Azure migration planning and execution
- A short technical review before a team commits to an AI architecture

### Jobs to avoid

- Commodity chatbot builds with no product owner or success metric
- Generic prompt-writing jobs
- Mass-produced no-code automation
- ML research or model training that requires a specialist data scientist
- Open-ended full-stack maintenance unrelated to AI
- Unpaid "test projects" or free architecture work
- Jobs where the client wants senior accountability at junior rates

## 6. Fast-entry offer ladder

The first objective is a small, real result that earns trust. It is not a cheap sample of a large engagement.

| Offer | Scope | Turnaround | Launch price |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Production AI Working Session | 90-minute architecture or debugging session plus a written decision summary | 24 hours | $600-$750 |
| LLM Production Health Check | Review one inference path, identify the top three risks, and deliver a prioritized action plan | 2 days | $1,500-$2,500 |
| RAG or Agent Hardening Sprint | Fix one bounded reliability, evaluation, latency, or tool-use problem | 1 week | $3,500-$7,500 |
| Azure AI Migration Plan | Target architecture, dependency map, sequence, risks, and acceptance gates | 3-5 days | $2,500-$5,000 |
| Migration Implementation Sprint | Execute an agreed migration with milestones and handoff | 1-3 weeks | From $7,500 |

If Upwork does not make Consultations available on the account, sell the working session as a normal fixed-price contract with one funded milestone.

For the first 25-30 proposals, publicly lead with only the Production AI Working Session and LLM Production Health Check. Use the larger offers when a reply or discovery conversation reveals the matching need. This keeps the initial buying decision simple without hiding broader capability.

### Best first-contract lane

Prioritize urgent, bounded problems such as:

- Repairing an OpenAI Responses API or tool-calling integration
- Adding structured outputs and validation to an unreliable workflow
- Diagnosing weak RAG retrieval and producing a measured fix
- Adding a small eval harness before a model change
- Tracing an LLM endpoint's latency by hop
- Adding a deterministic fallback or guardrail to one critical path
- Reviewing an Azure OpenAI or Azure AI Search design before implementation

Take a sub-$1,000 contract only when the scope can genuinely finish in one working day and the acceptance criterion is explicit. Do not accept a disguised application build.

## 7. Proposal templates

The template is structure, not copy to send unchanged. Rewrite the first two sentences for every job.

### Template A: debugging or production issue

> Hi {client name},
>
> Your {specific symptom} may be coming from {one plausible technical hypothesis}, but I would verify {specific signal} before changing the architecture.
>
> I can handle this in {timeframe}:
>
> 1. Reproduce and instrument the failing path
> 2. Isolate the root cause and agree on the smallest safe fix
> 3. Implement it and leave you with a short verification note
>
> Relevant proof: I architected and led a full Vercel/GCP-to-Azure production migration for a YC-backed startup in 7 days with zero customer-visible downtime. My current work centers on production AI architecture, agents, retrieval, evaluations, and Azure.
>
> One question before I scope it: {the single question that changes the approach or price}?
>
> Yenson

### Template B: new RAG or agent build

> Hi {client name},
>
> The important constraint in this build is not the chatbot UI; it is {retrieval quality / tool reliability / latency / privacy / evaluation}. I would make that measurable before expanding the workflow.
>
> Based on your post, I would propose three milestones:
>
> 1. Confirm the success criteria and failure cases
> 2. Build the thinnest end-to-end path with tracing and evaluation
> 3. Harden the agreed path, document it, and hand it over
>
> I have built production conversational AI systems since 2022 and now work across OpenAI, RAG, agent orchestration, guardrails, evals, Next.js, Python, and Azure architecture.
>
> Is {specific unknown} already decided, or would you like that included in the first milestone?
>
> Yenson

### Template C: architecture review

> Hi {client name},
>
> You appear to be deciding between {option A} and {option B} under {named constraint}. That is a good fit for a short architecture review because the valuable output is a defensible decision and sequence, not a generic report.
>
> I would review the current path, pressure-test the tradeoffs across quality, latency, cost, privacy, and operability, then deliver an annotated architecture and prioritized decision memo.
>
> I support startup teams with production AI architecture through Microsoft for Startups via Accenture, and I led a YC-backed startup's seven-day production migration to Azure with zero customer-visible downtime. My independent work is separate and non-conflicting.
>
> What decision date is driving this review?
>
> Yenson

### Template D: short invitation response

> Thanks for the invitation. This looks aligned with my work in {specific overlap}. The first thing I would verify is {specific check}, because it determines {cost / architecture / delivery consequence}. If you can share {one input}, I can suggest a bounded first milestone before we meet.

### Apply when

- The post describes a concrete system, symptom, decision, or deadline
- At least 70% of the core stack is familiar
- The client names a production or business consequence
- The scope can be turned into a first funded milestone
- The budget is plausible, or the problem is valuable enough to justify a direct scope question
- You can write a specific technical opening without pretending to know the root cause

### Skip when

- The post is a feature wishlist with no owner, user, or success criterion
- It requests multiple unpaid screens, a free proof of concept, or speculative work
- The budget and requested seniority are structurally incompatible
- The client wants one person to be an AI researcher, designer, mobile developer, DevOps team, and growth marketer
- More than 20-50 proposals already exist unless your fit is unusually exact
- You would need to learn the core domain during the contract rather than at its edge

### Five-minute pre-proposal check

1. What exact outcome is the client buying?
2. What is the highest-risk unknown?
3. Which one portfolio proof is relevant?
4. Can the work start with a paid milestone under one week?
5. What single question would materially change scope or price?

If those answers are vague, skip the job. More proposals are not the same as more pipeline.

## 9. Seven-day launch plan

### Day 1

- Publish the title, overview, rate, skills, certifications, and employment history
- Set availability accurately
- Upload portfolio item 1 with a sanitized architecture visual
- Create saved searches and notification rules

### Day 2

- Upload portfolio items 2 and 3
- Submit 5 highly specific proposals to jobs posted recently
- Favor one-day fixes, reviews, and production debugging over greenfield applications

### Days 3-7

- Submit 5-8 qualified proposals per day
- Apply early where possible; do not apply merely to hit a quota
- Track post age, proposal count, opening angle, reply, interview, and outcome
- Respond to messages within a few hours during stated working time
- Offer the paid working session when the full scope is unclear
- Follow up once when a client engaged and then went quiet; do not chase cold proposals

### Metrics after the first 25 proposals

| Metric | Healthy early signal | If weak |
|---|---:|---|
| Profile views from proposals | 25%+ | Improve the first two proposal lines and job selection |
| Replies or interviews | 10%+ | Make the technical observation more specific and narrow the target jobs |
| Paid first milestones | 1-2 | Reduce approval friction with the working session, not a lower hourly rate |

These are operating targets, not guarantees or Upwork benchmarks. Use the first 25 proposals as your own baseline.
